What notebook 05 runs and in what order (top), what each experiment's model computes on one face image (middle), and the full experiment plan (bottom). Amber steps are opt-in and do nothing until you set a flag.

1. What notebook 05 runs, step by step

The same notebook trains any experiment: choose them in EXPERIMENTS (Step 2) and each one is saved in its own outputs/<experiment>/ folder. Everything up to the model check runs automatically; training and the final test score only run when you switch them on.

2. What each model computes on one image

Every experiment uses the same pipeline: a 224×224 face is normalized, turned into features by a backbone, pooled into one number per feature, passed through dropout and a new one-output linear head, and a sigmoid turns that score into P(fake). Training minimizes BCEWithLogitsLoss with AdamW. What differs is the backbone, which parts learn, how fast, and (E6 only) what the training photos look like.

E1 · Baseline CNN trained from scratch01_baseline_cnn.yaml

No pretrained knowledge: a small 4-block CNN starts from random weights and learns everything from the 100,000 training faces. It answers how far a network gets with no prior knowledge.

E2 · EfficientNet-B0, frozen backbone02_effnet_frozen.yaml

The pretrained backbone is used as a fixed feature extractor: only the new one-output head learns. It answers whether features learned on everyday ImageNet photos are enough on their own.

E3 · EfficientNet-B0, full fine-tuning (main model)03_effnet_finetune.yaml

Every layer learns from the first step, but the pretrained backbone learns 10× more slowly than the new head so its ImageNet knowledge is adjusted gently rather than overwritten.

E4 · EfficientNet-B0, gradual unfreezing04_effnet_gradual.yaml

Same model as E3, trained in two phases: the head first learns alone on top of the frozen backbone, then the whole network is fine-tuned. The idea: a random head cannot send useful gradients into the backbone at the start, so let it settle first.

E5 · ResNet-50, full fine-tuning05_resnet50_finetune.yaml

Same training recipe as E3, with a different and much larger pretrained backbone. It answers whether the architecture matters.

E6 · EfficientNet-B0, robust training (damage-aware)06_effnet_robust.yaml

Exactly the E3 model and recipe. The only change is the training input: half of the training photos get one random kind of real-world damage, so the model learns to cope with poor-quality uploads.

3. The experiment plan

Words used in the table. Clean = the original real and fake images, with no damage added. Degradation-aware training = training with damaged photos: each training photo (real or fake) has a 50% chance of getting one random kind of damage (blur, JPEG, downscale, noise or screen capture) at a random severity 1–5, made on the fly. It does not mean "no damaged photos"; it means the model is shown damaged photos while it learns, so it gets used to poor-quality uploads. Only E6 trains this way; E1–E5 train on clean photos. The validation photos always stay clean (real and fake images).

Why E6 exists: E3 and E6 are identical except for damage during training. Testing both on the same damaged photos (notebook 03 does this for E3 and E6 by default) shows whether degradation-aware training really makes the model more robust. Set INCLUDE_DAMAGE = True in notebook 03 to test the other models on damage too.

How to read it. LR = learning rate of the new last layer; the pretrained backbone learns 10× slower (E1 has no pretrained part). Warm-up = epochs in which the learning rate rises slowly from 0. Frozen epochs = epochs in which the backbone does not learn (E2: the whole run). Early stop = training stops if the validation loss has not improved for this many epochs in a row; the best epoch is kept. All experiments use seed 42, AdamW, dropout 0.2, 224×224 images and mixed precision on a GPU.

The notebook scores models on the validation split while you choose settings; the test split is used once, at the end (Step 9). These are research classifiers for images in a benchmark dataset; they do not establish identity or prove fraud on their own.
