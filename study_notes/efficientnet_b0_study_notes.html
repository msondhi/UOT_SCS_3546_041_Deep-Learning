1. The four key words

WordWhat it isEveryday analogy
EfficientNet-B0An architecture: the design of the network (its layers and how they connect). On its own it is just a template full of random numbers.The wiring of a brain
ImageNetA dataset: 1.28 million everyday photos in 1,000 classes (dogs, cars, cups…). It contains no real-vs-fake faces.The school textbook
Pretrained weightsThe numbers EfficientNet-B0 ended up with after other researchers trained it on ImageNet. They encode general "seeing": edges, textures, shapes, eyes.What the brain learned at school
Fine-tuningTaking that pretrained model, replacing its last layer and training further on our 140k real and fake faces.A job course for that educated brain
EfficientNet-B0 is the model. ImageNet is the dataset its starting weights came from. We never trained on ImageNet ourselves; we start from weights others learned there, then train on our face dataset.

2. What pretrained=True really downloads

In src/deepfake_kyc/models.py:

timm.create_model("efficientnet_b0", pretrained=True, num_classes=1, drop_rate=0.2)

Downloaded?Size
ImageNet images (1.28 M photos)No, neverabout 150 GB, separate licence
EfficientNet-B0 weights learned on ImageNetYes, one file of numbersabout 20 MB
  1. Download the weights file once (cached afterwards).
  2. Load those numbers into the EfficientNet-B0 layers.
  3. num_classes=1 throws away the 1,000-class ImageNet last layer and adds a new, random, one-output layer for real vs. fake.

The images were the teacher; the weights are the knowledge the student kept. We only need the knowledge, and even if we had the images they would not teach real vs. fake, because ImageNet has no such labels.

One more link to ImageNet: every photo is ImageNet-normalized (colours adjusted with ImageNet's average colour statistics) because the pretrained weights expect images prepared the same way.

3. Weights vs. neurons vs. connections

WordPlain meaningEfficientNet-B0, one 224×224 photo
Weights (parameters)The learned numbers, like connection strengths. Saved in best.pt.4,008,829 in our version (5.3 M in the original with its 1,000-class layer)
Neurons (activations)The values calculated for one photo as it flows through; gone afterwards.about 834,000 at the stage outputs alone; millions counting every inner layer
Connections usedMultiply-and-add operations for one photo.about 385 million

Why more connections than weights? A convolution filter is a small stamp (for example 3×3) whose same weights are reused at every position of the image. So 4 million weights are used hundreds of millions of times per photo. This weight sharing is what makes CNNs efficient.

The pattern: the network trades "where" for "what". Early layers see fine detail over a large area; deep layers see a few big, abstract patterns. Inside: a stem, 7 stages with 16 MBConv blocks in total, a 1×1 head convolution, pooling, and finally our one-output linear layer.

5. Interactive 2: neurons and a shared filter

A tiny version of one feature map: an 8×8 grey image instead of a 224×224 colour photo, and one 3×3 filter (9 weights) instead of 32 filters with 27 weights each.

Filter

What to notice

  1. Each output cell is one neuron. It looks at its own 3×3 patch, multiplies by the weights, adds them up (weighted sum), then applies an activation function (SiLU in EfficientNet). The box under the diagram shows this math.
  2. The filter never changes while it slides. All 36 neurons share the same 9 weights. That is what "neurons in the same feature map share one filter" means.
  3. Different filters detect different patterns. Vertical edge lights up the left and right sides of the bright square; horizontal edge lights up its top and bottom.
This toyReal EfficientNet-B0 stem
8×8 grey image224×224 colour photo (3 channels)
1 filter, 9 weights32 filters, 27 weights each (3×3×3)
1 feature map, 36 neurons32 feature maps of 112×112 neurons, about 401,000 neurons
Moves 1 pixel at a timeMoves 2 pixels at a time, so 224 becomes 112
So it is not right to say "EfficientNet-B0 has no neurons, only filters". It has neurons arranged in feature maps, and the filters are the weights those neurons share.

6. Worked example: the 928 weights of the stem

  • 32 filters × (3 × 3 pixels × 3 colours) = 32 × 27 = 864 filter weights.
  • BatchNorm adds a scale and a shift per filter: 32 × 2 = 64.
  • Total: 864 + 64 = 928 learned numbers.

These 928 numbers are fixed after training and used on every image in step one. They only change during training, when the model learns better values. For each photo, each filter's 27 numbers slide across the image and produce one feature map; 32 filters produce 32 maps.

  • E2 (frozen): ImageNet features learned "what objects look like", not the tiny pixel and texture artifacts that reveal fakes, so training only the last layer cannot get far. It still beats random guessing, and it shows the backbone must be fine-tuned.
  • E3, E4, E6 are pretrained models whose weights we change (fine-tune). Only E1 starts from random weights.
  • Where weights change in the code: src/deepfake_kyc/engine.py, train_one_epoch: loss.backward() works out how much to change each weight; optimizer.step() applies it. The optimizer and its two learning rates (backbone ×0.1, head full) are set up in src/deepfake_kyc/train.py.

8. Common mix-ups and sentences for the report

Don't sayBecause
"We trained on ImageNet."We only reused weights from ImageNet training done by others.
"ImageNet detects fakes."ImageNet has no fake faces; detection comes from our training on the 140k faces.
"EfficientNet-B0 = pretrained."It is just the design; "pretrained on ImageNet" is a separate choice (pretrained: true).
"We download ImageNet images."Only a ~20 MB file of weights is downloaded.
"E3, E4, E6 are not pretrained."They start from ImageNet weights and fine-tune them.
"There are no neurons, only filters."Neurons exist; filters are the weights they share.

"We use the EfficientNet-B0 architecture initialized with weights pretrained on the ImageNet dataset (from the timm library). We replace its 1,000-class output layer with a single real/fake output and train it on the 140k Real and Fake Faces dataset."

"With pretrained=True we download only the EfficientNet-B0 weights learned on ImageNet, not the ImageNet images. These weights provide general visual features (edges, textures, facial parts)."

"EfficientNet-B0 has about 4.0 million weights in our version. Through weight sharing in its convolutions, it performs about 385 million multiply-add operations per 224×224 image."

"EfficientNet-B0 is a convolutional neural network: its neurons are arranged in feature maps, and neurons in the same map share one filter's weights."

Numbers were counted from the project's model code (timm EfficientNet-B0 with a one-output head, 224×224 input).

9. Reading P(fake)

P(fake) is how sure the model is that the face is fake (AI-generated).

P(fake)Model's answer
0.00100% sure it's real
below 0.5real
0.5 or morefake
1.00100% sure it's fake
That's why the "false accept" rows in notebook 04 are mistakes. Those faces were fake, but the model gave them P(fake) = 0.00, so it was completely sure they were real.
